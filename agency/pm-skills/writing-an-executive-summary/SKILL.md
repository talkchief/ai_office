# Writing an executive summary

The CEO asked for something and does not want to read the work to find out whether they got it. Your summary is the answer: what was asked, what exists now, where it is, and what to decide next. Written so it can be read in under three minutes and acted on without opening a single task.

## 1. Read before writing
- The CEO's own words: the brief, the purpose, the charter. What they asked for is the yardstick, not what the teams found interesting.
- Every finished task: what it delivered, the file it produced, the numbers in it, the review that approved it.
- The artifacts: the files themselves, by name. A deliverable nobody can open is not delivered.
- The live things: a published page, a URL, a database row, a mailbox, an account. These are what the CEO actually uses.

## 2. The shape
Around 300 to 500 words in total. Never longer than one screen.

- **What was asked** (1–2 sentences). The CEO's request in their own words, shortened, not reinterpreted.
- **What was delivered** (the body). Each thing by name, with where it is and what it is for. Quantify: how many pages, which sections, what it covers, what it measures. Put what the CEO will use first, working files last.
- **What it means** (2–4 sentences). The consequence for the business: what is now possible, what changed, what it cost in time. Bold the implication, never the label.
- **What is open** (only when true). Anything asked for that is not there, anything that needs a decision, anything that will break without attention. Say it plainly; a summary that hides a gap is worse than no summary.
- **Next** (2–3 lines, only when there is a real next step). The action, who would own it, and by when.

## 3. The rules
- Name the link. If the work produced a URL, a page, a file the CEO opens, it goes in the summary with its address, not "see the task". This is the single most common failure.
- Every claim comes from a finished task or a file. Never describe intent, plans or what a team said it would do. If it was not delivered, it does not appear except under what is open.
- Numbers come from the work, never invented. No number is better than a made-up one.
- No task ids, no team process, no "the lead approved". The CEO cares what exists, not who touched it.
- Decisive and factual. No hedging, no praise of the teams, no closing pleasantry.
- One idea per sentence. Plain words.

## 4. Check before answering
- Could the CEO use the result from this summary alone, without opening anything else?
- Is every thing the CEO asked for either delivered here or named under what is open?
- Is every link and file name exact, so it can be clicked or found?
- Is it under 500 words?
