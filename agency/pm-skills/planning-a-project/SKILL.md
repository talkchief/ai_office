# Planning a project from a brief

The CEO writes what to build or achieve and attaches what exists. You plan the whole project; the CEO adjusts afterwards. Plan once, completely.

## 1. Read everything first
- The brief, word for word: what is wanted, for whom, by when, what must happen, what must not. Keep the CEO's own words for the musts.
- Every attached document: numbers, names, constraints and decisions live there. Cite the document when you use one.
- The company: each team's purpose and people. A task goes to the team whose purpose and people fit; when two fit, the one whose lead owns the outcome.

## 2. Name and purpose
- A short name a person would say out loud.
- The purpose in one or two sentences: what it is for and what "done" looks like, measurable where the brief allows.

## 3. The charter (plain prose)
- Scope and what is out of scope.
- Objectives and how success is measured (numbers from the brief or the documents, never invented).
- Constraints: deadlines, sign-offs, budgets, compliance, anything that must or must not happen.
- Standards: which existing notes, templates or skills the teams must follow.

## 4. Milestones
- Two to six, in order, each a state of the world the CEO can verify ("beta live with three clients"), not an activity.
- Each with a date inside the project's window; the last one is the target date. Use the CEO's deadline when there is one; otherwise propose a realistic target and say why.
- The first milestone must be reachable with what exists today: the documents, the Brain, the teams' own knowledge.

## 5. Task briefs
- One to four tasks per milestone; each for one team, each a complete assignment: the deliverable by name (a file, a page, a decision), what it must contain, what to read first, the acceptance criteria, and what it feeds next.
- Tasks of the same milestone run in parallel; tasks of later milestones wait for the milestone before them.
- Anything the CEO must sign off is a task that ends in a request for that sign-off, never an assumption.

## 6. Check before answering
- Every must from the brief appears in the charter or a task.
- No task needs a tool or a team the office does not have; say so in the charter when something must be bought or hired.
- The first milestone's tasks can start now.
